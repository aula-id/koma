\section*{What happened}

On Saturday, a NORAD F-16 intercepted a civilian aircraft that had entered restricted airspace near President Trump's Mar-a-Lago resort in Palm Beach, Florida. U.S. military officials said the intercept happened at about 12:20 p.m.\ ET. Trump was in West Palm Beach that day to vote in Florida's midterm election.

The jet flew close in front of the civilian plane to get the pilot's attention. NORAD calls that standard tactic a ``headbutt maneuver.'' It worked: the crews made contact, and the pilot left the restricted airspace as instructed.

The plane had violated a Temporary Flight Restriction over the area. NORAD said the pilot could also be met by federal authorities on landing and face enforcement action.

\section*{Why the airspace was closed}

The FAA issues temporary flight restrictions for sporting events, severe weather, rallies, and other gatherings involving high-profile officials, especially the president. Pilots are expected to check Notices to Airmen (NOTAMs) for the current limits.

\section*{The day's political context}

Trump and First Lady Melania Trump were seen Saturday afternoon at a West Palm Beach polling station, where he cast an early ballot ahead of the following week's midterms. Asked if he voted along party lines, he said, ``Very much so.'' He then left for a campaign rally in Georgia.

His midterm rallies have been triggering similar restrictions. Earlier this month, NORAD F-16s intercepted a plane over Clemson, South Carolina, during a rally for Darline Graham's Senate campaign. Days before that, the command reported a similar intercept over Cincinnati, Ohio, near another rally he attended.
